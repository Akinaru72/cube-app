Positioning the Corners

There are two algorithms for moving the corners of the top layer.

The first algorithm R' F' L' F R F' L F moves the corners in the following order: (B,R) → (U,R) → (U,L). The back-left corner (B,L) remains in its original position.

R' F' L' F
R F' L F

The second algorithm F' L' F R' F' L F R moves the corners in the opposite direction. The back-left corner (B,L) also remains in place.  Using these two algorithms, you can easily place every corner into its correct position.

Orienting the Corners

To completely solve the cube, you must correctly orient at least two, and sometimes all four corners of the last layer.

For this, use the algorithm we already applied at the beginning of this guide: R F' R' F or F' R F R'.

Remember that performing either of these algorithms six times in a row returns the cube to its original state. Also, four repetitions of one algorithm are equivalent to two repetitions of the other (see the beginning of the guide).

How to Apply the Algorithm

Rotate the top layer until the corner that needs to be oriented is in the (F,R) position — the front-right corner.

Perform one of the algorithms above until the corner is correctly oriented.

Then rotate the top layer again and bring the next unsolved corner into the (F,R) position. Repeat the process until every corner is correctly oriented.

While performing the algorithm, it may seem that the cube is getting scrambled. Ignore this. After each cycle of six moves, it returns to a valid state.
